# Running useful customer interviews

Sample note written for this repository. Original text, generic advice.

The goal of an early interview is to learn how a person handles a problem today. It is not to get them to approve of your product. Approval is cheap and tells you little, because most people are polite to someone who is visibly excited about an idea.

## Before the call

Write down the three things you most need to learn. For example: how often the problem occurs, what it costs when it goes wrong, and who decides to spend money on fixing it. If you cannot name three, you are not ready to book the call.

## During the call

- Ask about the last time the problem happened. A specific recent event produces facts. A general question produces opinions.
- Ask what they did about it, what tools they used, and how long it took.
- Ask what they have already paid for or built to deal with it. Money and effort already spent are the strongest evidence that a problem matters.
- Stay quiet after a question. The second half of an answer is often the useful half.
- Hold the product description until the end, if you give it at all.

## After the call

Write notes within an hour. Separate what the person said they did from what they said they would do. Only the first group counts as evidence.

## Warning signs

- The person agrees with everything and offers no detail.
- They describe the problem as annoying but cannot recall when it last happened.
- They suggest features without having tried any workaround.

Ten interviews with people in one narrow role teach more than thirty spread across many roles.
