# Outbound basics for a first sales motion

Sample note written for this repository. Original text, generic advice.

Outbound means contacting people who have not asked to hear from you. It works when the list is narrow and the message is about the reader.

## Build a small, specific list

Start with fifty to one hundred people who share one job title in one type of company. A narrow list lets you write one message that fits everyone on it. A broad list forces a vague message that fits nobody.

For each person, record one fact that explains why you are writing to them now. Examples: a new store opening, a job posting that mentions the problem, a recent change of software.

## Write a short first message

- Two or three sentences in plain text.
- The first sentence refers to the fact you recorded.
- The second names the problem in the words that people in that job use.
- The last asks a question that is easy to answer, such as whether this is something they are working on this quarter.

Leave out attachments and feature lists.

## Follow up

Most replies come after the second or third message. Send two or three follow ups spaced several days apart. Each one should add something new, such as a short example or a relevant number. Stop after that and revisit the contact in a few months.

## Measure

Track messages sent, replies, positive replies, and meetings booked, by list and by message version. If fewer than one in twenty people reply at all, the list or the problem statement is likely wrong. Fix those before editing the wording.

## Protect deliverability

Send from a separate domain, warm it up slowly, and keep daily volume low per mailbox.
