# Runway math

Sample note written for this repository. Original text, generic advice.

Runway is the number of months a company can operate before it runs out of cash.

## The basic calculation

Net burn is the cash that leaves each month minus the cash that comes in.

    runway in months = cash in the bank / monthly net burn

A company with 400,000 in the bank, 50,000 in monthly costs, and 10,000 in monthly revenue has a net burn of 40,000 and ten months of runway.

## Use cash, not accounting profit

Annual contracts paid up front, unpaid invoices, and deferred payroll taxes all make the cash picture differ from the income statement. Runway is a cash question, so use the bank balance and actual payment dates.

## Plan with three cases

- Base case: current costs and current revenue, with no growth assumed.
- Planned case: the hires and spending you intend to make, with revenue you have already signed.
- Bad case: planned spending with revenue that arrives three months late.

If the bad case leaves less than six months, delay a hire or cut a cost now. Waiting removes options.

## Fundraising timing

Raising money usually takes several months from first meeting to cash received. Starting with nine or more months of runway leaves room for a slow process. Starting with four does not.

## Can revenue cover costs in time

Project revenue forward at its current growth rate and compare it with costs month by month. If revenue passes costs before the cash runs out, outside money is a choice. If it does not, the company depends on investors, and the plan should say so plainly.

Review the numbers on a fixed day every month.
