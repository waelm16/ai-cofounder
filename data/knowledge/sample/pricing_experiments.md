# Pricing experiments for an early product

Sample note written for this repository. Original text, generic advice.

Early pricing is a guess that you improve through small tests. Nobody can tell you the right number in advance, including the buyer.

## Pick a unit first

Decide what the customer pays per: per seat, per location, per transaction, or a flat fee. The unit should rise as the customer gets more value. If a customer with fifty locations pays the same as one with two, the unit is wrong.

## Run the test in real sales conversations

State a price out loud and watch what happens. Useful reactions:

- Immediate acceptance with no questions. The price is probably too low.
- Questions about terms, contract length, or what is included. The price is in a workable range.
- The buyer needs to involve finance or a senior manager. You have crossed an approval threshold. Find out what that threshold is, because it shapes your packaging.
- Silence, then a change of subject. Either the price is far too high or the problem is not painful enough.

## Change one thing at a time

If you change the price, the unit, and the package together, you cannot tell which change caused the reaction. Keep a simple log with the date, the prospect, the price quoted, and the response.

## Common mistakes

- Offering a discount before the buyer asks for one.
- Running a free pilot with no end date and no agreed success measure.
- Setting price from cost alone. Cost sets your floor. It says nothing about what the result is worth to the buyer.

Raise the price for the next prospect after three acceptances in a row.
