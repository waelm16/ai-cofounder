# Unit economics basics

Sample note written for this repository. Original text, generic advice.

Unit economics describe whether a single customer makes or loses money over the time they stay. A company can grow revenue quickly and still lose more with each sale.

## The four numbers

- Acquisition cost: all sales and marketing spending in a period divided by the number of new accounts won in that period. Include salaries, tools, and travel.
- Gross margin: revenue minus the direct cost of serving the customer, such as hosting, support, and onboarding labour.
- Monthly churn: the share of accounts that cancel in a month.
- Average revenue per customer per month.

## Two checks

Payback period is the number of months of gross margin needed to recover the acquisition cost.

    payback months = acquisition cost / (monthly revenue per customer x gross margin)

If acquisition costs 6,000, monthly revenue is 1,000, and gross margin is 75 percent, payback is eight months.

Lifetime value is the gross margin earned over the expected life of a customer. Expected life in months is roughly one divided by monthly churn. At 2 percent monthly churn, expected life is about fifty months.

## Reading the results

- Payback under twelve months is comfortable for most small software companies. Beyond twenty four months the company needs a lot of capital to grow.
- Lifetime value several times larger than acquisition cost leaves room for error. A ratio near one means growth destroys cash.

## Cautions at an early stage

With fewer than twenty accounts, these figures are rough. Founder time spent selling is a real cost even when unpaid. Churn measured over three months understates long run churn. Treat the numbers as a way to spot problems early, and recalculate each quarter.
